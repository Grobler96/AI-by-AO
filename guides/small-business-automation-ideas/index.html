10 things a small business can automate this month

By the AI by AO team · Published 28 October 2026

You do not need a big project to get hours back. These ten jobs are repetitive, rules-based and easy to automate, which is exactly what tools like n8n are good at. Here is what to automate and how to choose where to start.

First, what is automation?

Business automation means a tool does a repetitive task for you, the same way every time. When X happens, do Y. A new enquiry arrives, a reply goes out and a record is created. n8n is a popular tool for connecting the apps you already use, such as email, calendars, spreadsheets, accounting software and your CRM, so they pass information to each other without you retyping it.

Ten things to automate

1. Instant replies to new enquiries

When a form is filled in or an email arrives, send a prompt, personal acknowledgement and log the details. Replying quickly is one of the biggest factors in winning work.

2. Quote follow-ups

Send a polite reminder a few days after a quote goes out, and another later. Most quotes go cold because nobody chases them.

3. Invoice creation and payment reminders

Create invoices from a completed job and send reminders as the due date approaches and passes, so you are not chasing by hand.

4. Appointment confirmations and reminders

Automatic confirmations and reminders reduce no-shows and save you the phone calls.

5. Review requests

After a job is finished, ask the customer for a Google review automatically. More reviews help you stand out locally.

6. Lead alerts

Get a message on your phone the moment a hot lead arrives, with their details attached, so you can respond while they are still interested.

7. A weekly summary report

Pull numbers from your tools into one email each Monday: enquiries, quotes sent, jobs booked, invoices outstanding.

8. New customer onboarding

When someone says yes, automatically send a welcome message, collect the information you need, create the folder and set up tasks for your team.

9. Tidying files and attachments

Save email attachments, signed documents and receipts into the right folders automatically, named consistently.

10. Content drafts for approval

Have AI draft a newsletter, social post or product description in your tone, then send it to you to approve before anything is published.

How to choose where to start

  • Pick the task you do most often. Small savings add up quickly.
  • Choose something rules-based. If you can describe the steps, a tool can follow them.
  • Start with one. Get it working and trusted, then add the next.
  • Keep a person in the loop for anything that goes to customers until you trust it.

What not to automate yet

Anything that needs judgement or a personal touch, such as sensitive customer conversations, or any process that is not yet written down or is still changing. Get the process clear first, then automate it.

Where AI fits in

Automation follows rules. AI adds the ability to read, summarise and draft: sorting incoming emails, writing a first reply, summarising a call, or answering customer questions. Questions people ask

How much time can automation save a small business?

It depends on how much repetitive admin you have. Many owners find a few hours a week disappear once enquiries, follow-ups and invoicing run on their own. The best way to find out is to list what you repeat every week.

Do I need to replace the software I already use?

Usually not. Automation tools connect to what you already use, such as your email, calendar, accounting software and CRM, so information moves between them without you retyping it.

Is automation only for bigger companies?

No. Small businesses often benefit most, because the owner is usually the one doing the admin. Starting with a single, simple automation is a sensible way in.
