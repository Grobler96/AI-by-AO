AI phone answering for trades: does it actually work?

By the AI by AO team · Published 28 October 2026

If you are on a roof, under a sink or in the middle of a job, you cannot answer the phone. AI phone answering promises to take those calls for you. Here is how it works, where it helps, and where it should hand over to a person.

The missed call problem

For a plumber, electrician, roofer or builder, a missed call is often a missed job. Customers who cannot get through usually ring the next name on the list. Voicemail helps a little, but many people never leave one.

What an AI phone caller does

An AI phone caller picks up the call straight away and talks to the customer in natural language. A well set up one can:

  • Greet the caller by your business name and find out what they need.
  • Ask the right questions, such as the job, the address, how urgent it is and a contact number.
  • Answer common questions about your services and the areas you cover.
  • Book a visit straight into your calendar.
  • Send you a short summary by text or email so you can call back when it suits you.
  • Pass urgent calls to you or a colleague straight away.

Where it works well

  • Routine enquiries: quote requests, booking a survey, asking if you cover an area.
  • Out of hours: evenings, weekends and when you are on a job.
  • Busy periods: it can take several calls at once without anyone being left on hold.

Where you still need a person

  • Emergencies: a burst pipe or no heating should reach you quickly. Good set ups recognise urgency and forward the call.
  • Complex or high-value quotes: the AI collects the details, and you handle the pricing conversation.
  • Upset customers: if someone is frustrated, a human should take over.

Making it sound right

The difference between a good and a bad AI caller is the set up, not the technology. It needs a script written around how you actually talk to customers, the right questions for your trade, your real service area and opening hours, and a clear rule for when to hand over. It should be tested with real test calls, including awkward ones, before it ever answers a customer.

The trust and legal basics

  • Be honest: a good set up lets callers know they are speaking to a virtual assistant if asked, and never pretends to be a named person.
  • Recording and data: if calls are recorded or transcribed, that is personal data under UK GDPR. You should tell callers, have a lawful basis, and keep the data only as long as you need it. The ICO publishes guidance worth reading.
  • Your privacy notice: update it so it covers how calls are handled.

How to set it up

  1. List the calls you miss most often and what the caller usually needs.
  2. Write the questions the AI should ask for each type of job.
  3. Decide what counts as urgent and who it should be forwarded to.
  4. Connect it to your calendar and a place to send call summaries.
  5. Test with real calls, then start with out-of-hours cover before widening it.

What affects the cost

Build effort (script, calendar and summary connections), a phone number or call forwarding, and ongoing call volume and usage. The number of call types you want it to handle matters most.

Is it right for you?

It is worth a look if you regularly miss calls while working, lose out-of-hours enquiries, or spend evenings returning calls. It is less useful if most of your calls are already complex conversations with existing clients. Questions people ask

Will customers know they are talking to an AI?

A well built voice assistant sounds natural, but it should not pretend to be a person. If a caller asks, it should say it is a virtual assistant, and it should hand over to a person when the situation needs one.

Can it book jobs straight into my diary?

Yes, when it is connected to your calendar. It offers real available slots, books one, and sends you the details. Many people start by having it take messages and add booking later.

What happens with an emergency call?

You decide the rules. A typical set up recognises urgent wording, forwards the call to your mobile or a colleague, and sends an alert. It should never leave an urgent caller with just a message.
