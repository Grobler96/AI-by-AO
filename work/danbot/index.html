DanBot: an in-house AI business agent for a workwear company

DanBot is an AI agent that lives inside ATAM Workwear's company dashboard. It reads the live business data, answers questions in plain English, warns the team about problems before they bite, drafts and sends emails, and can even ring the owner with a morning briefing. It is a lot more than a chat widget, and this is how detailed it is.

The business

ATAM Workwear decorates garments with embroidery and print for businesses across the UK. Orders, quotes, customers, stock, invoices and suppliers all live in different places, and a small team has to keep every one of them moving.

The challenge

The information a manager needs to make a decision is spread over many screens. Which orders will ship late? Which quotes have gone quiet? Which customers should be rung this week? Which supplier invoice does not match its purchase order? Finding out meant clicking through several tabs, and the things that were slipping were the things nobody had time to look for.

The team needed one place to ask, one that already knew the business, only ever showed each person what they were allowed to see, and never did anything important without a clear yes.

What we built

  • A business agent inside the dashboard that people talk to in plain English. It decides what to look up, checks the live figures and answers in short, direct sentences, never from guesswork.
  • An understanding of the whole business: open and late orders, production, quotes that need chasing, customers overdue to reorder, new customers, sales and supplier invoices, purchase orders, stock levels, monthly revenue and reminders.
  • Proactive alerts that flag what matters, such as late orders, a customer reply nobody has answered, and quotes going cold, plus a written morning briefing.
  • Guided help on a record. Press "Ask DAN" on an invoice and it explains why it was flagged, quotes the real numbers, and walks through the next steps one at a time.
  • Email drafting. It writes a chase for a quiet quote, or a welcome or win-back email, reads the whole message back, changes it on request, and sends only after a clear yes.
  • Confirmed actions such as logging a call, parking or reopening a customer, reassigning an order and setting or snoozing reminders.
  • A phone line, so the owner can ask questions and get the morning briefing by phone.

How detailed the build is

Most chatbots answer questions from a page of text. DanBot is closer to a colleague who has read every record. It is a build with real depth:

  • It decides what to look up. Instead of a fixed report, it chooses which data to pull, filters and groups it for totals and breakdowns, and combines several areas to connect the dots, such as an overdue customer who also has a quote out.
  • It knows the business rules. It has been taught how quotes are judged quiet, cold or lost, how reorder cycles work, how invoice checks and tolerances work, and which accounts need a purchase order number.
  • It adapts to the moment. Different behaviour for a chat, a guided walkthrough, an alert, a briefing and a phone call, including how it phrases things when they are spoken aloud.
  • It has been refined through 17 versions, with each release adding capability and tightening its behaviour.

Built to be trusted

An agent that can act inside a business has to be safe. That shaped most of the work.

  • Sign-in and permissions. Every person signs in, and it reads and acts only with that person's own access, exactly as the dashboard does.
  • A clear yes first. Before any action it reads back exactly what it will do and waits for a confirmation. Emails are read out in full before they go.
  • Some things stay human. It never approves, rejects or posts invoices, and never changes prices. Those stay a click for a person.
  • Honest failure. If it cannot read something, it says so rather than claiming there is no data. It never invents numbers, and it says plainly when something is outside what it knows.
  • Data is data. Text found inside records, notes or customer messages is treated as information, never as instructions.
  • Phone safety. The phone line only accepts approved numbers and asks for a spoken passphrase before it will act.
  • Limits. Each person has a usage limit, so costs and misuse stay under control.

The outcome

The team now has one place to ask about the business, and the agent raises problems without being asked. We have not published figures for this project.
